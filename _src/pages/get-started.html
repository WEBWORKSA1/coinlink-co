1 · The 27-point safety checklist

  1. Write your seed phrase on paper or metal — never in photos, notes apps, email or cloud drives.
  2. Store the backup in two separate secure places.
  3. Never type your seed phrase into any website, form, chat or “support” tool.
  4. Use a hardware wallet for anything you couldn't afford to lose.
  5. Buy hardware wallets only from the manufacturer's official site.
  6. Keep a separate “hot” wallet with small balances for new apps and mints.
  7. Bookmark every exchange and wallet site; use only the bookmarks.
  8. Never click wallet or exchange links in search ads.
  9. Turn on app-based or hardware-key 2FA — not SMS — on every exchange.
  10. Use a unique password per exchange, stored in a password manager.
  11. Enable withdrawal address allowlists on exchanges that offer them.
  12. Set an anti-phishing code so you can recognise genuine exchange emails.
  13. Use a dedicated email address for crypto accounts.
  14. Keep your devices and browser up to date.
  15. Install wallet extensions only from the official site's link to the store.
  16. Read every wallet prompt: “approve”, “permit” and “setApprovalForAll” grant spending rights.
  17. Be most suspicious of “unlimited” approvals.
  18. Review and revoke old token approvals every few months.
  19. Verify contract addresses on an explorer before swapping new tokens.
  20. Send a small test transaction before large transfers.
  21. Double-check the first and last 6 characters of every pasted address.
  22. Ignore unsolicited DMs — real support never messages first.
  23. Assume any “guaranteed return” or “double your crypto” offer is a scam.
  24. Don't install remote-desktop software at anyone's request.
  25. Keep records of every buy, sell and transfer for taxes.
  26. Tell a trusted person how to access your crypto in an emergency (without sharing the seed now).
  27. When in doubt, run the link through the CoinLink checker.

3 · The 9 phishing patterns behind most drains

  1. Search-ad clones — paid ads above the real result that lead to a pixel-perfect copy.
  2. Fake airdrop / claim pages — “you're eligible” sites that request a wallet signature.
  3. Wallet “sync” or “validation” — pages asking for your seed phrase to fix an error.
  4. Impersonated support — accounts that DM you after you post a question publicly.
  5. Hacked social accounts — real project accounts posting fake mint or claim links.
  6. Typosquats — one-letter changes like “uniswaap” or look-alike characters.
  7. Address poisoning — tiny transfers from look-alike addresses that end up in your history.
  8. Fake apps and extensions — clones in app stores with stolen branding.
  9. Romance / “pig-butchering” platforms — a new friend introduces a trading site that won't let you withdraw.

4 · “I clicked a bad link” — the first 10 minutes

  1. Close the site. Do not sign anything else.
  2. If you entered a seed phrase: create a new wallet on a clean device and move everything immediately.
  3. If you signed an approval: revoke it at revoke.cash on every affected network, then move funds.
  4. If you entered an exchange password: change it, reset 2FA, and check withdrawals and API keys.
  5. Document everything (links, addresses, transaction hashes) and report to Chainabuse and your national fraud centre.
  6. Beware “recovery” services that contact you — they are almost always a second scam.
