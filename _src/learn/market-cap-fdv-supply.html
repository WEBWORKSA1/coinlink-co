Two coins at the same price can be worth wildly different amounts. To compare crypto assets sensibly you need a handful of metrics — and you need to know what each one does and doesn't tell you. These are the same columns you'll see in our markets table.

Three kinds of supply

  • Circulating supply — tokens currently available to trade.
  • Total supply — tokens that exist now, including locked ones held by teams, investors or treasuries, minus any burned.
  • Max supply — the hard cap that can ever exist, if there is one. Bitcoin's is 21 million; many tokens have no max supply, and some have ongoing inflation.

Market capitalization

Market cap = price × circulating supply. It's the standard way to rank assets. A token trading at $0.10 with 100 billion circulating has a $10 billion market cap — larger than a $50 token with 100 million circulating ($5 billion). This is why “cheap” per-coin prices are meaningless on their own.

Market cap is not the amount of money invested. Thin order books mean a relatively small amount of buying can move the price — and therefore the market cap — a lot.

Fully diluted valuation (FDV)

FDV = price × max supply (or total supply if there's no cap). It answers: what would the valuation be if every token existed and traded at today's price? A large gap between market cap and FDV — sometimes called “low float, high FDV” — means many tokens are still to come. As they unlock, they can dilute holders and add selling pressure.

Token unlocks and vesting

Teams and early investors typically receive tokens on a vesting schedule: a lock-up (“cliff”), then gradual releases. Large unlock dates are public in most projects' tokenomics documents. They don't always cause price drops, but they change supply and deserve a look before you buy.

Volume and liquidity

  • 24h volume — the value traded in the last day across tracked exchanges. Some exchanges inflate volume, so treat outliers with caution.
  • Volume ÷ market cap — a rough activity gauge. Extremely low ratios suggest illiquidity.
  • Liquidity / order-book depth — how much you could buy or sell near the current price. A large market cap with shallow liquidity can mean painful slippage.

Other useful numbers

  • All-time high (ATH) and distance from it — useful context, not a target.
  • Dominance — an asset's share of total crypto market cap; Bitcoin dominance is a common gauge of risk appetite. See the sentiment dashboard.
  • TVL (total value locked) for DeFi protocols — deposits in smart contracts, which can include double-counting.

A quick research checklist

  1. Market cap and rank — how big is it really?
  2. FDV gap and upcoming unlocks — how much dilution is ahead?
  3. Volume and liquidity — could you exit?
  4. Official links — are you looking at the real project? Check the coin's profile on CoinLink.
  5. Concentration — do a few wallets hold most of the supply? Check holders on an explorer.

FAQ

Can a coin's market cap go up without new money coming in?

Yes. Market cap is just last price × supply. A small trade at a higher price raises it, and a token unlock increases circulating supply.

Is a low market cap coin more likely to grow?

It has more room in theory but also far higher risk of failure. Most small-cap tokens from previous cycles lost most or all of their value.
