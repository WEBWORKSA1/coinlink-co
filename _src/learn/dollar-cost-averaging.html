Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is the simplest disciplined strategy in investing: pick an amount, pick a schedule, and buy no matter what the market is doing. In a market as volatile as crypto, it's a popular way to build a position without agonizing over timing.

A worked example

Say you invest $100 a month for three months in a coin whose price goes $10 → $5 → $10.

MonthPriceInvestedCoins bought
1$10$10010
2$5$10020
3$10$10010
Totalavg $8.33$30040

Your average cost is $300 ÷ 40 = $7.50 — below the $8.33 average price. That's the core mechanic: a fixed dollar amount automatically buys more when prices are low. At the end, your 40 coins are worth $400. Had you invested $300 on day one at $10, you'd have 30 coins worth $300.

When lump sum wins

If prices mostly rise, investing everything immediately captures more of the gain, because money waiting on the sidelines misses the climb. Long-run studies of traditional stock and bond markets — for example by Vanguard — found lump-sum investing beat DCA roughly two-thirds of the time. Crypto is more volatile, which can narrow that edge, but the principle holds: DCA is primarily a risk and behaviour tool, not a guaranteed return booster.

Why DCA still makes sense for many people

  • It matches how most people earn money — monthly paychecks.
  • It reduces regret. A lump sum invested just before a 60% crash can push people to sell at the bottom.
  • It enforces discipline. Automated buys remove the temptation to chase pumps or panic in dips.

Setting up a low-cost DCA plan

  1. Choose the asset carefully. DCA into a coin that goes to zero still goes to zero. Many people limit DCA to large, established assets.
  2. Compare recurring-buy fees. Convenience features can charge 1–2% per purchase plus a spread. Use the fee calculator.
  3. Pick a frequency. Weekly or monthly both work; more frequent buys smooth more but may cost more in fees.
  4. Decide where coins live. Withdraw periodically to a wallet you control rather than leaving everything on an exchange.
  5. Keep records of every purchase for taxes.

FAQ

Should I stop DCA when prices are high?

The point of DCA is to avoid timing decisions. Some investors use value-averaging variations, but these reintroduce judgement calls.

Is DCA a guarantee against losses?

No. If the asset declines over your whole investment period and doesn't recover, you lose money.
