If Bitcoin is digital money, Ethereum is a shared global computer. Launched in 2015, it lets developers deploy programs — smart contracts — that anyone can use and no one can quietly change. Tokens, stablecoins, decentralized exchanges, lending markets and NFTs are all built this way.

Accounts, ETH and smart contracts

Ethereum has two kinds of accounts: externally owned accounts controlled by a private key (your wallet) and contract accounts controlled by code. Ether (ETH) is the native currency used to pay for computation and to secure the network. Most other assets on Ethereum are tokens following standards such as ERC-20 (fungible tokens) and ERC-721/1155 (NFTs).

Proof of stake

In September 2022, Ethereum switched from mining to proof of stake in an upgrade called the Merge, cutting its energy use dramatically. Validators lock up ETH as collateral and take turns proposing and attesting to blocks every 12-second slot. Misbehaviour can be punished by slashing part of their stake. Users who don't run a validator can stake through pools or liquid staking tokens, each with its own risks.

What gas is

Every operation a transaction performs — transferring a token, updating a balance, running contract logic — costs a fixed number of gas units. A simple ETH transfer uses 21,000 gas; a token swap might use 100,000–200,000 or more. Gas measures work, not money.

How the fee is calculated

Since an upgrade in 2021 (EIP-1559), the fee works like this:

Fee = gas used × (base fee + priority fee)
  • Base fee — set automatically by the protocol based on how full recent blocks were. It rises when demand is high and falls when it's low. The base fee is burned, permanently removing that ETH.
  • Priority fee (tip) — an extra amount you add to incentivize validators to include your transaction sooner.
  • Gas limit — the maximum gas you allow a transaction to use. If it runs out, the transaction fails but the gas spent is still paid.

Prices are quoted in gwei (one billionth of an ETH). At 2 gwei, a 21,000-gas transfer costs 42,000 gwei = 0.000042 ETH. Try our gwei converter and live gas tracker.

How to pay less

  • Use a layer 2. Rollups such as Arbitrum, Optimism and Base batch transactions and post data to Ethereum, often reducing fees to cents. See layer 2s explained.
  • Transact off-peak. Base fees tend to be lower when fewer people are active.
  • Batch actions. Consolidate transfers and avoid unnecessary approvals.
  • Don't overpay tips. Wallet “standard” settings are usually enough unless you need speed.

Safety notes specific to Ethereum

  • Smart contracts can have bugs; audits reduce but don't eliminate risk.
  • Token approvals let contracts move your tokens — review them. Read token approvals explained.
  • Fake tokens can copy names and logos. Verify contract addresses on an explorer or the project's verified site.
  • When adding networks to your wallet, use trusted network lists rather than links from strangers.

FAQ

Why did my transaction fail but still cost gas?

Validators did the work of executing it until it failed (for example, the gas limit was too low or a price moved beyond your slippage), so the gas for that work is still charged.

Is staking ETH risk-free?

No. Risks include slashing, smart-contract bugs in staking protocols, liquid staking tokens trading below ETH, and lock-up periods during exits.
