Bitcoin's monetary policy is written in code: new coins are created at a predictable, shrinking rate until roughly the year 2140, when the 21 million cap is reached. The mechanism that shrinks issuance is the halving.

How the halving works

Miners who add a block receive a block subsidy of newly created bitcoin plus the fees from transactions in the block. Every 210,000 blocks, the subsidy halves. Since blocks arrive about every 10 minutes, that's roughly every four years. No vote or company decides this — every node enforces it automatically.

Why it matters

Supply

Each halving cuts the flow of new bitcoin entering the market. After 2024, about 450 new BTC are created per day, down from about 900. More than 19.5 million of the 21 million coins already exist, so new issuance is a small and shrinking fraction of supply.

Miners

A halving cuts miners' subsidy revenue by half overnight. Less efficient miners may shut down until difficulty adjusts. Over the long term, transaction fees must make up a larger share of miner revenue to keep the network secure.

Price narratives

Past halvings were followed by major bull markets, which is why the event attracts attention. But there have been only four, macro conditions differed each time, and expected events tend to be priced in. Treat the “halving cycle” as a narrative, not a law.

Common questions

  • Does the halving affect my coins? No. Balances and transactions are unchanged; only the reward for new blocks changes.
  • Do other coins halve? Some Bitcoin-derived coins, such as Litecoin, have similar schedules at different times.
  • What happens after the last halving? Around 2140 the subsidy reaches zero and miners are paid by fees alone.

FAQ

Can the halving schedule be changed?

Only by a change to Bitcoin's consensus rules that the overwhelming majority of users and node operators adopt. The 21 million cap is considered one of Bitcoin's most fundamental properties.

Are “halving giveaway” promotions real?

No. Scammers use high-attention events like halvings to promote fake giveaways and “bonus” sites. Check links with our link checker.
