Reference

Crypto glossary

Short, accurate definitions of the words you'll meet in crypto — with the security angle included where it matters.

ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPRSTUVWZ

A

Address
A public string that identifies where crypto can be sent, like an account number. Safe to share; it can't be used to spend funds.
Address poisoning
A scam where tiny transfers from a look-alike address land in your history so you copy the wrong address later. Always check the full address.
Airdrop
Free tokens distributed to wallets, often to reward early users. Fake “claim” sites are a top phishing tactic — see common scams.
Allowance
The amount of a token a smart contract is permitted to move from your wallet after an approval.
Altcoin
Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin.
AML
Anti-money-laundering rules that require regulated platforms to monitor and report suspicious activity.
AMM
Automated market maker: a DEX that prices trades with a formula over pooled liquidity instead of an order book.
Approval
A transaction granting a smart contract permission to spend a token from your wallet. Review and revoke old ones.
APR
Annual percentage rate — yearly return without compounding.
APY
Annual percentage yield — yearly return including compounding. See the staking calculator.
ATH
All-time high: the highest price an asset has ever traded at.
ATL
All-time low: the lowest price an asset has ever traded at.
Audit
An external security review of smart-contract code. Reduces, but never removes, risk.

B

Bag holder
Someone left holding an asset after its price collapsed.
Base fee
The protocol-set portion of an Ethereum gas fee, which is burned.
Bear market
A prolonged period of falling prices.
Block
A batch of transactions added to a blockchain together.
Block explorer
A website for looking up blocks, transactions, addresses and contracts. See how to use one.
Block height
The number of blocks before a given block; used to measure chain length and schedule events like halvings.
Block reward
New coins plus fees paid to the miner or validator who adds a block.
Blockchain
A shared, append-only ledger where each block links cryptographically to the previous one.
Bridge
A protocol that moves assets between blockchains, often by locking and minting wrapped tokens. A frequent hack target.
Bull market
A prolonged period of rising prices.
Burn
Permanently removing tokens from circulation by sending them to an unusable address or destroying them in code.

C

CEX
Centralized exchange: a company-run trading platform that holds customer funds.
Circulating supply
Tokens currently available to trade. Used to calculate market cap.
Cold wallet
A wallet whose keys are kept offline, usually on a hardware device.
Confirmation
Each block added after the one containing your transaction; more confirmations mean more finality.
Consensus
The rules and process by which network participants agree on the valid state of a blockchain.
Custodial
A service that holds the private keys on your behalf, such as an exchange account.

D

DAO
Decentralized autonomous organization: a group governed by token holders' votes executed via smart contracts.
dApp
Decentralized application: a front end that interacts with smart contracts.
DCA
Dollar-cost averaging: buying a fixed amount on a schedule. See the DCA guide.
DeFi
Decentralized finance: financial services run by smart contracts rather than intermediaries.
Depeg
When a stablecoin or pegged asset trades meaningfully away from its target value.
DEX
Decentralized exchange: trades directly from your wallet via smart contracts.
Difficulty adjustment
Bitcoin's automatic retargeting every 2,016 blocks to keep block times near 10 minutes.
Dominance
An asset's share of total crypto market capitalization.
Drainer
Malicious code or site that tricks you into signing approvals or transfers that empty your wallet.
DYOR
“Do your own research.” Good advice — and also a phrase scammers use to deflect responsibility.

E

EIP
Ethereum Improvement Proposal: a formal design document for changes to Ethereum.
ERC-20
The standard interface for fungible tokens on Ethereum and EVM chains.
ERC-721
The standard for non-fungible tokens (NFTs) on Ethereum.
ETF
Exchange-traded fund; spot crypto ETFs hold the actual asset on investors' behalf.
EVM
Ethereum Virtual Machine: the runtime for smart contracts, shared by many compatible chains.
Exit scam
When a project's team disappears with investors' funds.

F

FDV
Fully diluted valuation: price × maximum (or total) supply. See market cap vs FDV.
Fear & Greed Index
A 0–100 sentiment score for the crypto market. Live on our sentiment page.
Fiat
Government-issued currency such as USD, EUR or INR.
Finality
The point at which a transaction cannot practically be reversed.
Flash loan
An uncollateralized loan that must be borrowed and repaid within one transaction; often used in exploits.
FOMO
Fear of missing out — the urge to buy because prices are rising fast.
Fork
A change to a blockchain's rules. A hard fork isn't backward-compatible and can split a chain.
FUD
Fear, uncertainty and doubt — negative sentiment, sometimes spread deliberately.
Funding rate
Periodic payments between long and short traders on perpetual futures to keep prices near spot.

G

Gas
The unit measuring computational work on Ethereum and EVM chains; fees are gas × price.
Gas limit
The maximum gas a transaction may consume.
Genesis block
The first block of a blockchain.
Gwei
One billionth of an ETH; used to quote gas prices. Convert with our unit converter.

H

Halving
Bitcoin's scheduled 50% cut to the block reward every 210,000 blocks. See halving explained.
Hardware wallet
A dedicated device that stores keys offline and signs transactions securely.
Hash
A fixed-length fingerprint of data produced by a one-way function.
Hashrate
The total computing power securing a proof-of-work network.
HODL
Holding crypto long-term rather than trading; from a famous typo of “hold”.
Honeypot token
A scam token coded so buyers can't sell.
Hot wallet
A wallet whose keys live on an internet-connected device.

I

ICO / IDO
Initial coin / DEX offering: early token sales to the public. High-risk and frequently abused.
Impermanent loss
The shortfall a liquidity provider can suffer versus simply holding, when pool token prices diverge.

K

KYC
Know your customer: identity verification required by regulated platforms.

L

Layer 1
A base blockchain such as Bitcoin, Ethereum or Solana.
Layer 2
A network that scales a layer 1 by processing transactions off-chain and settling to it. See L2s explained.
Leverage
Borrowing to increase position size; magnifies gains, losses and liquidation risk.
Lightning Network
A Bitcoin layer for fast, low-cost payments through payment channels.
Liquid staking
Staking through a protocol that gives you a tradable token representing your staked position.
Liquidation
Forced closure of a leveraged or borrowed position when collateral falls below a threshold.
Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price.
Liquidity pool
Tokens locked in a smart contract to enable trading on an AMM.

M

Market cap
Price × circulating supply. The standard way to rank crypto assets.
Memecoin
A token driven mainly by internet culture and community rather than utility. Extremely volatile.
Mempool
The waiting area of unconfirmed transactions. Bitcoin fees are visible on our fees dashboard.
Mining
Competing to add blocks in proof-of-work systems in exchange for rewards.
Mint
To create new tokens or NFTs.
Multisig
A wallet requiring several keys to approve a transaction, e.g. 2-of-3.

N

NFT
Non-fungible token: a unique token often representing art, collectibles or access rights.
Node
A computer running blockchain software that validates and relays data.
Nonce
A counter used once — e.g. an account's transaction number on Ethereum, or the value miners vary in proof of work.

O

Off-ramp / On-ramp
Services that convert crypto to fiat (off) or fiat to crypto (on).
On-chain
Recorded directly on a blockchain and publicly verifiable.
Oracle
A service that brings off-chain data, such as prices, onto a blockchain for smart contracts.
Order book
The list of buy and sell orders on an exchange at different prices.

P

P2P
Peer-to-peer: directly between users without an intermediary.
Paper wallet
Keys printed on paper. Largely outdated and easy to mishandle.
Passphrase
An optional extra word added to a seed phrase that creates a separate hidden wallet.
Permit
An off-chain signature granting a token allowance without a transaction; abused by drainers. See approvals explained.
Perpetual futures
Futures contracts with no expiry, kept near spot price by funding rates.
Phishing
Tricking users into revealing credentials or signing malicious transactions, usually via fake sites. Check links with our checker.
Pig-butchering
A long-con romance or friendship scam that leads victims to a fake trading platform.
Private key
The secret that controls a crypto address. Whoever holds it controls the funds.
Proof of reserves
An attestation that an exchange holds assets covering customer balances.
Proof of stake
A consensus method where validators lock collateral to propose and attest to blocks.
Proof of work
A consensus method where miners expend computing power to add blocks.
Public key
Derived from a private key; used to create addresses and verify signatures.
Pump and dump
Coordinated buying to inflate a price, followed by insiders selling to latecomers.
Punycode
An encoding for non-Latin domain names (xn--…) that can hide look-alike characters.

R

Rebase token
A token whose supply adjusts automatically, changing balances in wallets.
Revoke
Cancelling a token approval so a contract can no longer move your tokens.
Rollup
An L2 that batches transactions and posts data or proofs to a layer 1.
RPC
Remote procedure call endpoint a wallet uses to talk to a blockchain node.
Rug pull
When developers abandon a project and take investors' funds, often by draining liquidity.

S

Satoshi (sat)
The smallest unit of bitcoin: 0.00000001 BTC.
Seed phrase
12–24 words that back up a wallet's keys. Never share or type it into a website.
Self-custody
Holding your own keys rather than trusting a custodian.
Sequencer
The operator that orders transactions on a rollup.
Sharding
Splitting a blockchain's workload into parallel pieces to scale.
Slashing
A penalty that destroys part of a validator's stake for misbehaviour.
Slippage
The difference between expected and executed trade price, larger in thin markets.
Smart contract
Self-executing code deployed on a blockchain.
Spread
The gap between buy and sell prices; a hidden trading cost.
Stablecoin
A token designed to track a reference asset, usually the US dollar. See stablecoins explained.
Staking
Locking tokens to help secure a proof-of-stake network in exchange for rewards.
Swap
Exchanging one token for another, typically on a DEX.

T

Testnet
A practice network using valueless tokens for development and testing.
Token
A digital asset issued by a smart contract on an existing blockchain.
Tokenomics
A token's supply, distribution, vesting and utility design.
Total supply
All tokens that currently exist, including locked ones, minus burned.
Transaction hash (TxID)
The unique ID of a transaction, used to look it up on an explorer.
TVL
Total value locked: assets deposited in a DeFi protocol's contracts.
Typosquatting
Registering misspelled domains of popular sites to catch mistyped visits.

U

Unlock
The release of previously locked tokens to team members or investors under a vesting schedule.
UTXO
Unspent transaction output: Bitcoin's model for tracking spendable coins.

V

Validator
A participant that proposes and attests to blocks in proof of stake.
Vesting
A schedule that releases tokens gradually over time.
Volatility
How much and how quickly a price moves.

W

Wallet
Software or hardware that manages keys and lets you send, receive and sign.
Wei
The smallest unit of ETH: 10-18 ETH.
Whale
An individual or entity holding a very large amount of an asset.
Whitepaper
A document describing a project's design and purpose. Bitcoin's was published in 2008.
Wrapped token
A token representing another asset on a different chain, e.g. wrapped BTC on Ethereum.

Z

Zero-knowledge proof
Cryptography that proves a statement is true without revealing the underlying data.
zk-rollup
A rollup that posts validity proofs to its layer 1 to prove batches are correct.
